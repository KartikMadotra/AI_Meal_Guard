A more competition-style title for the report:

AI MealGuard: An AI-Based Age-Aware System for Visual School Meal Assessment, Nutritional Monitoring and Meal Quality Improvement

I like AI MealGuard because it sounds like an actual system/product rather than just a school project.

🧠 1. THE CORE IDEA

The system answers one main question:

“Is this meal nutritionally appropriate for this student, and if not, what needs to be improved?”

Instead of manually checking every plate, MealGuard uses AI to:

Identify student → understand age → scan meal → identify food → estimate quantity → calculate nutrition → compare with requirements → score meal → explain result → suggest improvement.

⚖️ 6. QUANTITY ESTIMATION

This is a major part of your project.

The AI doesn't just say:

“Dal detected.”

It tries to estimate:

“Approximately 80 g of dal.”

The system uses:

standardized plate,
fixed camera position,
known plate dimensions,
segmentation,
food area/volume estimation,
calibration information.

Output:

Rice       ~150 g
Dal        ~80 g
Vegetable  ~55 g
VERY IMPORTANT

We should call these estimates, not exact measurements.

The system should say:

Estimated quantity: ~80 g

rather than:

Quantity: 80.000 g

That makes your project scientifically much more defensible.

This controls things like:

Allowed alternatives

This is important because AI shouldn't decide the nutritional rules itself.

AI answers:

“What is on the plate?”

The databases answer:

“What does it contain?”

The requirements database answers:

“What is appropriate for this age?”

The scoring engine answers:

“How well does it meet the selected criteria?”

🧮 11. SCORING ENGINE

The score could combine several components.

For example:

              MEAL SCORE
                  │
       ┌──────────┼───────────┐
       ↓          ↓           ↓
   Nutrition   Portion    Required
   adequacy    adequacy   components
       │          │           │
       └──────────┼───────────┘
                  ↓
             SCORE / 100

A possible initial framework:

Protein adequacy       30%
Energy adequacy        25%
Meal components        20%
Micronutrients         15%
Portion adequacy       10%

These weights are project-design choices, not official government scoring unless we find a source that explicitly defines them.

We can refine this once we lock the exact standard.

🟢🟡🔴 12. PASS / REVIEW / FAIL

I strongly recommend three levels, not just pass/fail.

🟢 PASS

Meal comfortably meets the selected criteria.

🟡 REVIEW

Meal is close to the threshold or the AI estimate has significant uncertainty.

🔴 FAIL

Meal clearly fails one or more critical criteria.

This is much more realistic because camera-based quantity estimation is approximate.

⚠️ 28. WHAT THE SYSTEM SHOULD NOT CLAIM

Don't say:

“AI proves the food is safe.”

A camera cannot establish microbiological safety.

Don't say:

“AI measures exactly 82 g.”

Say:

“AI estimates approximately 82 g.”

Don't say:

“This is an official government score.”

Unless your scoring system is actually based on an official published scoring standard.

Instead:

“MealGuard Score based on configured nutritional and meal criteria.”

This makes your project much more credible.
